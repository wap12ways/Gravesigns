# Professional Astrologer — Pre-Session Preparation Labor

An ordered, phased inventory of the tasks a credentialed astrologer performs between booking and a live in-person consultation. Every phase notes the kind of authority that supports it. Where a task is standard technical repertoire taught across accredited curricula but not verbatim in a single fetched page, that is stated plainly rather than over-attributed.

> Compiled by web research restricted to gold-standard / accredited sources (NCGR, ISAR/CAPISAR, the Faculty of Astrological Studies, OPA) and named authorities (Kelly Surtees, Frank Clifford, Sue Tompkins, benebell wen). See "Sources consulted" at the end.
>
> **Headline caveat:** no accredited body publishes a fixed prep-time figure. The "4–6 hour" number is a practitioner norm — credible as an upper-range, rectification-inclusive estimate, not an association-mandated standard. See "Time budget" at the end.

---

## Phase 1 — Intake & Data Verification

- **Collect exact birth data** — date, precise clock time, and place (city/lat-long) of birth; confirm the time source (birth certificate vs. memory vs. hospital record) and grade its reliability, because an inaccurate time invalidates the Ascendant, houses, and all timing work.
- **Take a pre-session intake / questionnaire** — capture the client's stated concerns, life situation, and what they want the session to address, plus their level of astrological literacy (so fundamentals aren't over-explained to an intermediate, or assumed for a novice).
- **Clarify scope, role, and third-party context** — if the reading is requested by or about a third party, define the astrologer's role and the limits of confidentiality up front (NCGR A.9).
- **Confirm logistics & informed consent** — communicate session length, frequency, and fees before any work begins (NCGR D.4, D.5a).
- **Trigger rectification when the time is unknown/uncertain** — reverse-engineer the likely birth time from documented life events:
  - Gather and grade the reliability of any partial birth records.
  - Build a timeline of major dated life events (moves, marriages, deaths, career turns).
  - Generate candidate birth times and test each against the events using angles, progressions, transits, profections, and returns; converge on the best-fitting Ascendant/MC.
  - Treat the result as provisional, since rectification "is not always conclusive."

**Sources:** NCGR Code of Ethics (A.9, D.4, D.5a — confidentiality, scope, fee/logistics disclosure); Faculty of Astrological Studies (rectification defined as finding the likely birth time from past events); The Astrology School *Birth Time Rectification Course* (concrete rectification workflow); Kelly Surtees / NORWAC lecture (talking with the client before the session; assessing their astrology level).

## Phase 2 — Chart Casting & Data Prep

- **Cast the natal chart** and verify the calculation (correct time zone, DST, coordinates, ephemeris data).
- **Choose and note the house system and zodiac** (tropical/sidereal), since the astrologer will state this to the client at the session's opening.
- **Cast all derived/predictive charts for the session window:**
  - Secondary progressions (and progressed Moon phase).
  - Solar arc directions.
  - Transits for the session date and the coming ~1–2 years.
  - Solar return (and, where relevant, lunar return) for the current year.
  - Eclipses and lunations hitting sensitive chart points.
  - As needed: relevant harmonics and midpoint structures.
- **Print / assemble the chart set** as clean working copies for annotation.

**Sources:** ISAR CAPISAR Competency domains explicitly cover chart calculation and forecasting via "progressions, returns, transits, eclipses, lunations"; Faculty of Astrological Studies Foundation curriculum (calculating/drawing the chart, astronomical basis); benebell wen and Kelly Surtees (practitioners: pre-set the house system/zodiac to state at the opening). Harmonics/midpoints are standard predictive repertoire within these curricula rather than a verbatim line on a single fetched page.

## Phase 3 — Technical Analysis Pass (the astrologer's worksheet)

- **Work the natal chart systematically and take written notes** — a professional does not interpret cold; they study the chart and "jot down key notes to anchor themselves" so they don't lose focus or ramble.
- **Assess the technical layer:**
  - Planetary condition — essential/accidental dignities, sect, retrogrades.
  - Aspects and major configurations (stelliums, grand trines/crosses, T-squares) — flag the distinctive patterns to highlight.
  - Chart shape/weighting and elemental-modal balance; dominant signatures.
  - Dispositors / chart ruler and the "way in" to the chart.
  - Lots/Arabic parts and fixed-star contacts where the astrologer's method uses them.
- **Build the timing picture** — lay out a dated timeline of progressions, solar arcs, transits, and returns with the interpretation of each, so forecasting is grounded in specific dates.

**Sources:** benebell wen and Kelly Surtees (study-and-note-taking discipline; flag uncommon configurations; interpretation checklist "to touch on the most important features … and in terms of timing"); Frank Clifford, *Getting to the Heart of Your Chart* (methodical "way in": begin, assess what's most important, prioritize); Sue Tompkins, *The Contemporary Astrologer's Handbook* (step-by-step synthesis of planets/signs/houses/aspects). ISAR Competency domains anchor the technical repertoire (dignities, symbolism, chart calculation, forecasting). Dignities/lots/fixed stars are standard curriculum content, cited here at the level the fetched sources support.

## Phase 4 — Synthesis & Theme-Building

- **Spot the repeating themes** across the chart rather than reciting placements — identify what recurs and prioritize it; avoid getting "lost in too much detail."
- **Narrow to 3–5 core themes / main talking points** and write a compact anchor outline (often a single page) that "guides the live reading."
- **Resolve contradictions** — reconcile conflicting testimonies into a coherent narrative.
- **Decide emphasis and restraint** — choose what to foreground and what to hold back, framed by the ethics rule to present interpretations "with objectivity and appropriate qualifying statements, rather than as final or unequivocal pronouncements," and to avoid statements that "could cause harm through confusion … or fear."
- **Sequence a narrative arc** — decide the reading structure (e.g., House-and-Sign sequential vs. Subject-by-Subject: personality, work, relationships, timing) and where the forecast fits.

**Sources:** Frank Clifford, *Getting to the Heart of Your Chart* (spot themes, prioritize, don't drown in detail); Sue Tompkins, *Contemporary Astrologer's Handbook* (synthesis method); benebell wen (single-page anchor outline; choose reading structure); NCGR Code of Ethics A.4a, A.2, A.5 (qualifying language; avoid fear/harm/manipulation — governs what to emphasize/withhold).

## Phase 5 — Client-Specific Research

- **Re-read the client's stated concerns** and map each to the relevant houses/rulers/transits so the reading answers their actual questions.
- **Tailor the depth** to the client's astrology literacy captured at intake.
- **Pull the transits/progressions specifically relevant to their situation** (e.g., a relationship or career question) and prepare the dated forecast around it.
- **Anticipate likely follow-up questions** and pre-locate the chart factors that address them, so the session stays responsive rather than a monologue.

**Sources:** ISAR CAPISAR Consulting Skills Training ("place the client in the center," "tune into the client's thoughts and feelings," and prevent listening from becoming secondary to information delivery); Kelly Surtees / NORWAC (talk with the client beforehand; manage expectations); benebell wen (decide overview vs. specific-subject focus based on what the client wants).

---

## Phase 6 — Rehearsal, Materials & Logistics

- **Prepare printed charts, handouts, and the written note/outline** the astrologer will read from during the session.
- **Prepare the timeline handout** (transits/progressions with dates and interpretations) for the client to take away.
- **Decide recording** — whether the session is recorded and, if so, set up capture (many training programs use recorded readings as standard practice).
- **Ready the consultation space** — the physical room/setting is treated as part of preparation ("handling the consultation space").
- **Prepare opening remarks** — the house system/zodiac used and how the session will run.

**Sources:** Faculty of Astrological Studies (Diploma-level "handling the consultation space and delivering a professional reading"; two recorded live client readings with supporting materials are built into the course); Kelly Surtees / NORWAC ("get you, the client's chart, and your space ready"); benebell wen (pre-level-set the system with the client).

## Phase 7 — Ethical & Self-Preparation

- **Review the code of ethics for the sensitive content in this chart** — competence limits (only work "within the boundaries of their competence," NCGR A.3); avoid harm, fear, intimidation, and emotional manipulation (NCGR A.2, A.5c-d); qualify predictions rather than deliver them as fixed fate (A.4a).
- **Set confidentiality and boundaries** — treat the session as confidential (NCGR B.1–B.2), and "maintain reasonable boundaries … with the best interests of the client in mind" (D.2).
- **Plan how to handle difficult disclosures** — prepare to deliver hard material (health, death, relationship, financial themes) responsibly; the OPA/ISAR consulting training explicitly rehearses "real-world client scenarios" and difficult-topic handling.
- **Center / ground before the session** — do the personal/energetic preparation to be "in optimal energetic form," including self-awareness about the astrologer's own chart in relation to the client's (transference/counter-transference).
- **Confirm informed consent and fees are settled** so the client understands what is and isn't being offered.

**Sources:** NCGR Code of Ethics (A.2, A.3, A.4a, A.5, B.1–B.2, D.2 — verbatim provisions); OPA Consulting Skills / Certification programs (ethics + "real-world client scenarios," reflective feedback); ISAR CAPISAR Ethics Awareness Training ("cornerstone" of certification) and Consulting Skills Training; Faculty of Astrological Studies (final module = consultation skills + ethics, with self-awareness developed through studying astrologer–client synastry); Kelly Surtees / NORWAC (energetic/personal readiness).

## Time budget (the "4–6 hours" claim, assessed honestly)

**No accredited association publishes a fixed prep-time figure.** The number is a practitioner norm, and fetched practitioner sources cluster somewhat lower than 4–6 hours for a routine session, rising into that range for a serious/complex one. A defensible breakdown built from the tasks above:

- Intake review + data verification: ~15–30 min (much longer, +1–3 hrs, if **rectification** is required — this is the single biggest swing factor).
- Chart casting + assembling all derived charts: ~30–60 min.
- Technical analysis worksheet (Phase 3): ~60–90 min.
- Synthesis / theme-building + one-page anchor outline (Phase 4): ~45–60 min.
- Client-specific tailoring + forecast timeline (Phase 5): ~30–60 min.
- Materials, handouts, recording setup, space, ethical/self-prep (Phases 6–7): ~20–40 min.

**Total: ~3–4.5 hours for a standard serious session, reaching 4–6 hours when rectification, a two-year forecast timeline, or a written handout package is involved** — plus post-session write-up/recording editing that some practitioners also count. Practitioner sources explicitly note prep time "is included in the price the client pays," and that the anchor outline (often a single page) is the distilled output of this multi-hour pass. Bottom line for encoding into GraveSigns: the 4–6 hour figure is credible as an upper-range, rectification-inclusive estimate, but it should be framed as a practitioner norm, not an association-mandated standard.
