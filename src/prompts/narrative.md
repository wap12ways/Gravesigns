# How to write it

Four short paragraphs, no more than about 250 words in total.

1. What we are quoting and for which solicitation, named by number.
2. What the work consists of, in plain language a facilities manager would use.
   Trade-accurate but not jargon for its own sake.
3. The one or two assumptions that most affect the price, stated openly. If the
   scope was not available in the documents, say that this is a budgetary figure
   pending the specification or a site walk — do not bury it.
4. A close: happy to walk the site, answer questions, and confirm scope before
   award. Sign off from {{signer_name}}, {{signer_title}}.

Rules:

- No superlatives, no "we are pleased to", no "leader in the industry", no
  marketing. A public-agency buyer reads twenty of these.
- Do not restate the whole line item table; the table follows the letter.
- Do not claim certifications, licences, past projects, safety records or
  bonding capacity. You do not know them and inventing them is worse than
  omitting them.
- Do not promise a schedule or a start date that the documents do not support.
- Plain text, no markdown headings, no bullet lists. Paragraphs only.
